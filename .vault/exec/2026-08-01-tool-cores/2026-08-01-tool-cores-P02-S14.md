# Close the two trust-structure findings the P01 review raised, as one change because they share a root

## Description

- Freeze both declaration structures behind read-only mappings, following the
  in-package precedent rather than inventing an idiom.
- Freeze entry interiors as well as membership, so declared arguments and flags
  are immutable rather than only the set of entries.
- Materialise wire shapes at the boundary so every downstream consumer sees the
  types it saw before and a caller mutating its own copy cannot reach back.
- Keep both composition-seam assertions, and correct what each of them is.

## Outcome

Both structures are immutable and the module no longer implies the seam
assertions are the enforcement when the constructor is.

The freeze went one level deeper than the row asked, and the reasoning carries
it: sealing membership while leaving an entry's flags and arguments writable
would be a freeze in name only, and the registry declares the arguments that
actually execute, so a mutable interior is a reachable interior. Materialising
wire shapes on the way out is what makes the deeper freeze safe rather than
merely stricter, and that is proven by test rather than asserted.

## Notes

This Step corrected the finding that produced it, and the correction matters
more than the freeze.

The finding treated the two seam assertions as one class and stated neither
could be reached by legitimate production input, offering deletion as an option.
That is true of one and false of the other. The construction seam validates that
a trust axis was DECLARED - present and boolean - and deliberately does not
constrain WHAT was declared. So an entry declaring no local-write protection is
perfectly constructible, and the local-write assertion is the only thing
deciding whether such an entry may reach a surfacing configuration. It is
enforcement. It cannot fire against today's registry solely because the single
shipped entry declares protection; a second entry declaring otherwise would trip
it immediately, with no code change. Taking the deletion option would have
removed live enforcement of a policy nothing else owns.

The network-reach assertion genuinely is redundant and stays as a labelled
backstop against a future second registry or a construction path that bypasses
the declared seam.

The premise was proven rather than argued: a test drives the real constructor
with an unprotected entry and shows it is admitted, which turns a reachability
claim into a checkable fact. The refusal recorded when the finding was first
written was honoured - no injection parameter and no registry patching to force
the guard end to end.

The audit carries this as a correction rather than a silent edit, because the
original finding was authored by this campaign. A finding that conflates two
mechanisms is the same defect class as code that does, and deserves the same
visibility.
